# Health service coverage: findings and recommended next step

**For:** the ministry. **Source:** WHO Global Health Observatory, UHC Service Coverage Index (SDG 3.8.1), 195 countries, 2000–2023. Scores run 0–100; higher is better. Figures are unweighted country averages.

*Scope note: the data is country-level. No facility-level outpatient data was available, so this brief ranks countries, not facilities.*

## Key findings

1. **Progress has slowed.** The average score rose from 55.3 (2000) to 66.2 (2015) but only to 68.4 by 2023, about 0.7 points a year before 2015 and 0.3 since. 72 of 195 countries made no gain between 2019 and 2023.
2. **The gap is wide.** The Africa average is 50.9 against 79.3 for Europe.
3. **Low coverage is concentrated.** 36 countries score below 50, and 27 of them are in Africa. 8 are rated Critical (below 40) and 28 High need (40–49).
4. **Service capacity and infectious diseases are the weakest areas** in those 36 countries (averages 31 and 39), compared with maternal and child health (50) and noncommunicable diseases (63).
5. **Three countries need attention first.** Ranking by how low the score is (70%) and how slow progress has been since 2015 (30%) gives:
   - **Chad:** index 26 against a regional mean of 51, barely moving (+1). Weakest area: service capacity and access (10).
   - **Gabon:** index 48, down 4 points since 2015, the largest decline of any country. Weakest area: infectious diseases (24).
   - **Guinea-Bissau:** index 43, down 2 points. Weakest area: infectious diseases (23).

   Madagascar, Ethiopia and Papua New Guinea also score very low but are improving, so they rank lower. They still warrant monitoring.

## Recommended next step

Within 30 days, convene a focused review for Chad, Gabon and Guinea-Bissau. Confirm the figures with each national health information team, identify the single weakest service area in each, and agree one costed action per country. In the same exercise, ask each country to submit facility-level outpatient data so the next version of the tracker can show which facilities are driving the gap.

## Caveats

- Values are whole numbers with no confidence intervals, so a 1-point change is within rounding.
- 29 countries have under 50% primary data availability; their scores rest more on modelled data.
- The index measures service coverage only, not financial protection (SDG 3.8.2) or quality beyond the tracer indicators.
- The priority ranking depends on the 70/30 weighting. It can be changed on the Assumptions tab of the tracker.
